With two-step verification on, the panel asks for a second factor after your password: a 6-digit
code from your authenticator app, or one of your [passkeys](./passkeys.mdx). The panel has root
access to the server, so turn it on for every administrator account.

## Turn it on with an authenticator app {#turn-it-on}

1. Open **Security** from your account menu.
2. In the **Two-step verification** section, press **Set up 2FA**.
3. Enter your password and press **Confirm**.
4. Scan the QR code with your authenticator app.
5. Enter the **6-digit code** the app shows and press **Verify and enable**.

If the code doesn't match, check that your phone's clock is right. Once verification is on, the
panel signs out your other sessions; the one you used stays open. A passkey works as a second
factor too: see [Add a passkey](./passkeys.mdx#add-a-passkey).

## Save your recovery codes {#recovery-codes}

The first time you turn on a second factor, the panel shows ten **recovery codes**, once. Each one
signs you in a single time in place of your code or passkey. Press **Download** or copy them, keep
them somewhere safe away from this device, then press **I saved them**.

To get a new set, press **Make new codes** in the **Recovery codes** section and confirm it's you.
The old codes stop working. The section shows how many codes you have left.

## Sign in {#sign-in}

After your email and password, the panel asks for your second factor:

- the **6-digit code** from your app. Each code works once: if you've just used one, wait for the
  next;
- **Use a passkey**, when your account has one and you opened the panel at its domain name;
- **Use a recovery code**, when your phone and passkeys aren't at hand.

After 10 wrong codes within an hour, the panel stops checking codes for that account until the hour
is up. See [two-step codes](/docs/explanation/sign-in-protection#two-step-codes).

## Confirm a change {#confirm}

Changing how your account signs in (setting up or replacing the app, turning it off, adding or
removing a passkey, making new recovery codes) opens a **confirm it's you** window: your password,
then a code, a passkey or a recovery code. Someone who gets hold of an open session can't swap your
factors without them.

## Switch to a new authenticator app {#replace-the-app}

1. In **Security**, press **Replace the authenticator app**.
2. Confirm with your password and a code from the old app. If the old phone is gone, choose **Use a
   recovery code** instead.
3. Scan the new QR code, enter a code from the new app and press **Verify and enable**.

The old app keeps working until you confirm.

## Turn it off {#turn-it-off}

1. In **Security**, press **Disable 2FA** and confirm.
2. Enter your password and a current code, then press **Disable 2FA**.

Turning off the app signs out your other sessions and **revokes your API tokens**. If you have no
passkey left, your recovery codes go too.

## Require it on the panel {#require-it}

An administrator decides who must use two-step verification:

1. Open **Settings**.
2. In the **Access** section, under **Require two-step verification**, pick **Not required**, **For
   administrators** or **For every account**.
3. Press **Save**.

An account that must have it and doesn't is sent to its **Security** page right after the password,
and sees nothing else until it sets up an app or a passkey. Its API tokens
are held back the same way.

## If you lose every factor {#lost-device}

Sign in with a recovery code and replace the app or the passkey. Without codes:

- an administrator can reset your two-step verification from **Users** (see
  [Reset someone's two-step verification](./users-and-roles.mdx#reset-two-step));
- for an administrator account, on the server, as root, run
  `panel-api recover-admin --email <your-email> --disable-2fa`. See
  [Recover administrator access](./recover-admin-access.mdx).
