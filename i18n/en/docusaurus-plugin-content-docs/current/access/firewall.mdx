The **Firewall** page, in the **Server** group, shows whether the firewall is active and its rules, as
the system reports them. Administrators add and remove rules; read-only accounts only see them.

When the firewall is active during installation, the installer opens ports 22, 80, 443 and 8443 over
TCP and 443 over UDP. The panel does not turn the firewall on by itself: when the page says
**inactive**, rules have no effect.

## Open a port {#allow-a-port}

1. Open **Firewall**.
2. In **Add or remove a rule**, choose the **Action** **Allow port**.
3. Type the **Port**, 1 to 65535.
4. To open it to one address only, type the IP or the network (for example `203.0.113.7` or
   `203.0.113.0/24`) into **Only from this IP**.
5. Press **Apply**.

From the panel, rules are always TCP.

## Block a port {#deny-a-port}

As above, with the **Action** **Deny port**. With **Only from this IP** you block the port for that
address only.

## Remove a rule {#delete-a-rule}

Choose the **Action** **Delete rule**, with the same **Port** and the same **Only from this IP** as
the rule to remove, and press **Apply**. The panel removes the allow and the deny rule of exactly
that shape, and leaves the port's other rules alone.

## The ports that stay open {#essential-ports}

The panel refuses any rule that would lock you or the sites out:

- the ports SSH listens on (usually 22);
- 80 and 443, the sites';
- 8443, the panel's.

For these ports you cannot remove the rule or block them for everyone. You can block them for a
single address, or add allow rules.

## From the API {#from-the-api}

`GET /api/firewall` reads the status. `POST /api/firewall/rule` with
`{"action": "allow", "port": 3306, "proto": "tcp", "from": "203.0.113.7"}` applies a rule; `action`
is `allow`, `deny` or `delete`, `proto` is `tcp` or `udp`. Every rule lands in the **Audit log**.
