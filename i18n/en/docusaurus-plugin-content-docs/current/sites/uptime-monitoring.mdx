With the uptime check on, the panel requests the site every minute from its public address, as a
visitor would. When the site stops answering, you get an alert.

The check starts off on every site: a site whose domain does not point here yet would look like an
outage.

## Turn the check on {#turn-it-on}

1. Open the site: the **Uptime** card is on the **Overview**.
2. In **Path to request**, enter a page that answers quickly. Leave `/` if you have no better page.
3. Press **Turn on**.

The first check runs at once. The card shows the state (**answering**, **one failure**, **down**), the
**Address checked**, the **Last check** and the **Answer**, with the HTTP status and the time. While
the site is down it also shows **Down since**.

To change the path, enter another one and press **Save**. To stop the check, press **Turn off**: an
open alert closes.

The path starts with `/` and is made of letters, digits and `. _ ~ -` between slashes, with no query.

## What counts as an answer {#what-counts}

The panel asks for `GET https://<domain><path>`, with a 10-second timeout.

- **Answering**: any status below 500, 401 or 404 included. The site is answering.
- **Failure**: a 5xx status, a refused or reset connection, a certificate a browser would refuse, no
  answer within 10 seconds.

The panel follows no redirect: a 3xx counts as an answer, and the check never leaves the site's
address.

## The alert {#the-alert}

Two failures in a row raise an alert of kind **Uptime**, at error level, with the reason of the last
failure. A single failure, such as a lost packet or a reload, is not enough. The alert shows in the
**Alerts** panel and goes to the alert webhook (see
[alerts and notifications](/docs/server/alerts-and-notifications)). It closes by itself when the site
answers again.

A restart of the panel neither closes nor reopens an alert that is already open.

## With the API {#with-the-api}

- `GET /api/sites/<id>/uptime` returns `enabled`, `path`, `url`, `interval_seconds` and `last`, the
  last check.
- `PUT /api/sites/<id>/uptime` with `{"enabled": true, "path": "/health"}` turns the check on or
  changes the path; `{"enabled": false}` turns it off.
