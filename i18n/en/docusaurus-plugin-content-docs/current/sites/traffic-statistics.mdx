Every site has visitor statistics, built on the server from its nginx access log. Nothing is added
to your pages: no script, no cookie, no third-party service. Visitors' addresses aren't stored.

## Open the statistics {#open}

1. Open the site.
2. Under the **⋯** menu of the tabs, choose **Traffic**.
3. Pick **7 days**, **30 days** or **90 days**.

The panel reads each site's access log every five minutes, so a new visit shows up within about
five minutes. A new site has statistics from the moment its first request is logged.

## What the figures mean {#figures}

- **Page views**: pages opened by people. Bots and scripts, images, stylesheets and other files,
  the WordPress admin, login and API, and requests that failed aren't counted.
- **Visitors**: distinct visitors per day, told apart by address and browser. Over several days the
  page adds up the days, so someone who comes back on two days counts twice.
- **Requests**: every request the site answered, including bots, with the share that came from
  bots and scripts.
- **Data sent**: the size of the response bodies.
- **Served from the cache**: the share of page-cache requests answered from the cache (see
  [cache behaviour](/docs/reference/cache-behaviour)).
- **Server errors**: 5xx responses, with the number of 4xx responses (not found or refused).

The chart shows page views and visitors per day, followed by page views for each of the last 48
hours. **Top pages** lists the 20 most viewed paths. **Not found** lists the paths that returned
404: broken links, or scanners looking for files.

Days are UTC days. The panel's own requests, such as the uptime check and cache warming, aren't
counted.

## Privacy {#privacy}

The agent reads the log and keeps only totals per day. To count distinct visitors it keeps one
small sketch per day, made from a salted hash of the address and browser. That sketch and its salt
are deleted after two days, leaving only the number. The panel receives no addresses. Nothing
is sent anywhere else.

The access logs themselves are unchanged and rotate as before (14 days kept). The statistics keep
90 days of totals.

## Use the API {#with-the-api}

`GET /api/sites/<id>/traffic?days=7` (also `30` or `90`) returns `days`, `hours` (the last 48),
`totals`, `top_pages`, `top_not_found` and `collected_at`.
