While the maintenance page is on, visitors get a page with your title and message and a `503` status,
not the site. Search engines read the `503` and `Retry-After` as "come back later". Nothing of the site is cached meanwhile.

## Turn it on {#turn-on}

1. Open the site and go to the **Protected paths** tab.
2. In the **Maintenance page** card, fill in what you want:
   - **Title**: plain text, up to 100 characters. Empty shows *Down for maintenance*.
   - **Message**: plain text, up to 2000 characters; line breaks are kept. Empty shows a default
     message.
   - **Come back in**: how many minutes visitors are told to wait, 1 to 10080. It is sent as
     `Retry-After`.
3. Click **Turn maintenance on**.

The title and the message are text: markup and `${…}` show as typed and are never run.

## Keep seeing the site {#allow}

Some visitors still get the real site:

- the addresses in **Addresses that see the site**: IP addresses or networks, one per line. Tick
  **Let my address through** to add your own;
- this server itself;
- any browser that opens the **Preview address**, shown in the card while the page is on. Opening it
  sets a cookie that lets that browser through for eight hours. Anyone who holds the address can do
  the same, so share it with care.

A visitor you [refuse](/docs/access/refuse-visitors) is still refused.

## Turn it off {#turn-off}

Click **Turn it off**. The panel empties the site's page cache, which held pages from before your
work, and warms it again in the background. The title and message are kept for next time.

## With cgctl {#cgctl}

```bash
cgctl maintenance <site-id> on "Back at 18:00"
cgctl maintenance <site-id> off
```

The message is optional; without it the saved text stays.
