CloudGround runs no mail server. PHP's `mail()` and WordPress's `wp_mail()` hand each message to the
panel, which sends it through an SMTP **relay** you choose. Order confirmations and password resets
from your sites leave through it, and so do the [alert emails](/docs/server/alerts-and-notifications#email).
The relay's password stays in the panel: the site never sees it.

## Set the server's relay {#server-relay}

1. Open **Settings** and find the **Mail** card.
2. Fill in **SMTP server**, **Security**, **Port**, **Username** and **Password**.
3. Fill in **Sender address**: the address messages come from when a site names none. Most relays
   accept only addresses on a domain you verified with them.
4. Click **Save**.
5. Type your own address next to **Send a test** and click it.

**Security** is one of:

| Choice | Port | Behaviour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **STARTTLS** (default) | 587 | The connection starts in plain text and is upgraded. A relay that does not offer it is refused: the panel never falls back to plain text. |
| **TLS** | 465 | Encrypted from the first byte. |
| **None** | 25 | No encryption. The panel refuses to send a password over it, unless the relay is this server itself. |

Leave **Port** empty to take the port of the chosen security. The test message goes through exactly
what a site's mail will use; if the relay refuses it, the panel shows the relay's answer.

## Give a site its own settings {#per-site}

Every PHP site sends through the server's relay. To change that for one site:

1. Open the site and go to the **Mail** tab (under **More**).
2. Set **Sender address**, for example `noreply@<your domain>`: WordPress then sends from this
   address instead of its default one. Empty means the relay's sender.
3. To use another relay for this site only, turn on **Use a relay of its own** and fill in its
   server, security, port, username, password and sender.
4. Click **Save**.

The banner at the top of the card says where the mail goes: through the site's relay, through the
server's, or nowhere. Turn off **Send mail from this site** and `mail()` fails: nothing is sent.
Only an administrator can save these settings; an operator assigned to the site can send the test.

A **staging copy sends no mail**, so a test never reaches a real customer.

## Check SPF, DKIM and DMARC {#checklist}

Receivers trust mail more when the sender's domain says that the relay may send for it. The
**SPF, DKIM and DMARC** card looks up three records of the sender's domain and shows each as
**found** or **missing**:

- **SPF**, a TXT record starting `v=spf1`, on the domain;
- **DKIM**, at `<selector>._domainkey.<domain>`: type the **DKIM selector** your relay gave you
  above, save, and click **Check again**;
- **DMARC**, at `_dmarc.<domain>`. A policy such as `p=none` is a good start.

The panel only says whether the records are there. Your relay's provider says what each must say.

## Limits {#limits}

- A site can send 200 messages an hour; beyond that its mail fails until the hour has passed.
- A message has at most 50 recipients and 10 MiB. A `Bcc` header is removed before it is sent.
- When mail fails, `mail()` returns false and the reason is in the panel's journal: the site, the
  number of recipients and the result, never the content or the addresses.
